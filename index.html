Overview

LLM agents change what misinformation is, and give us new ways to study it

A single model can fabricate a plausible claim. An agent can refine that claim against feedback, tailor it to an audience, push it through a social network, and gradually change what people believe. The same agents also give researchers a laboratory in which the spread of falsehoods, the drift of beliefs, and the effect of corrections can be simulated, measured, and stress-tested before they play out on real platforms.

This tutorial covers recent work in this area through three questions. How do agents generate misinformation, from unintended hallucination to goal-directed fabrication and the co-evolution of generators and detectors? How does misinformation propagate as LLM personas spread, distort, and polarize around content, and when can such simulations be trusted to reflect real populations? How can agents intervene, through evidence retrieval, multi-agent fact-checking and debate, and correction strategies, and how reliable are the verifiers themselves?

Throughout, we focus on what works, what fails, and how to tell the difference: agreement among agents does not guarantee correctness, accurate fact-checking does not always change minds, and a simulation needs validating before its conclusions are trusted.

Who should attend

Researchers, graduate students, and practitioners in NLP, information retrieval, social computing, trustworthy AI, and multi-agent systems.

Prerequisites

Basic familiarity with machine learning, NLP, and large language models. No prior experience with agents or social simulation is assumed.

Outline

Three parts, one pipeline

  1. Part I ~70 min

    Misinformation Generation

    • LLM-generated misinformation: mechanisms and methods
    • Goal-directed misinformation generation with agents
    • Adversarial co-evolution of generators and detectors

    From hallucination and source distortion to agents that draft, evaluate, and revise narratives, and detectors that must keep up.

  2. Part II ~70 min

    Agent-Based Propagation Simulation

    • Modeling users, social networks, and interactions
    • Content evolution, belief dynamics, and polarization
    • Evaluation and validation of social simulations

    How LLM personas pass content along, how it mutates, how beliefs shift, and how to check a simulation against real data.

  3. Part III ~70 min

    Agent-Based Verification and Intervention

    • Evidence retrieval and multi-agent fact-checking
    • Misinformation correction and intervention strategies
    • Reliability and dynamic evaluation of verification agents

    Agents that retrieve evidence, debate, and write corrections, and the failure modes that make verifiers unreliable.
